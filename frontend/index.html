Which forest is this, and what does the answer cost?

Six classifiers label 30 m cells of Colorado's Roosevelt National Forest from terrain, water, road and soil data. Accuracy is half the story; this dashboard also measures what each model spends to get it, and what happens when it meets a wilderness area it has never seen.

Classify a location

Set the terrain, pick a model, and see its answer next to all the others. Latency is measured on the server for this single prediction.

Biome-locked test

The four wilderness areas stand in for biomes. Each model is scored twice per area: trained and tested inside the area, and trained on the other three then tested on the held-out area. The gap is the price of leaving home.

Right: share of each cover type per area. Areas differ in which species they contain at all, so a model cannot name a class it never saw in training.

Method and limits

Limits

The input is tabular, not multimodal; the study covers one forest in one region; wilderness areas are a proxy for biomes; and a single train/test split is complemented, not replaced, by cross-validation on a subsample. Latency was measured on a CPU-only desktop and will differ elsewhere.
